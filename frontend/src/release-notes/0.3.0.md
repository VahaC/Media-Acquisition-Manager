## Highlights

**Schedule the replacement of a copy you already have.** When a release is a better copy of a film
you have, or of episodes of a series you have, the **Download** dialog's new **Replace after
verification** lets the owner tick exactly which of the old copies it should replace. The app writes
the replacement down with the download, before anything reaches qBittorrent, follows it until the
download is done, and then checks the new file. Nothing is moved or removed in this version, and
both copies stay: a later version moves the old copy aside, once its new file is verified, into a
quarantine you can restore it from. See [Scheduling a replacement](#scheduling-a-replacement).

**The new file is checked before anything is touched.** Once the download is done, the app checks
each new file by itself: that qBittorrent finished it, that it is a video the app can read, and that
Jellyfin, asked to look at it, has read it and identified it as the very film or episode your old
copy is — by Jellyfin's own ids, never by a file name. Each copy you ticked must pair with exactly
one new file. Jellyfin gets an hour for that; if anything is still missing then, or is wrong or
unclear sooner, the replacement stops and says why, and both copies stay. See [Checking the new
file](#checking-the-new-file).

**The owner's, and off until the owner switches it on.** After the upgrade replacement is off and no
folder is ticked. The owner switches it on, and ticks the library folders it may touch, on the
**Media folders** page; everyone else keeps **Keep both**, as before. See [Turning replacement
on](#turning-replacement-on).

**See what stands in the way before you tick.** Each of your copies says whether it can be replaced
and, if not, why; whether a torrent in qBittorrent still holds it, and what you will have to do
about that; and whether moving it would free any room at all. See [What the dialog tells you about
each copy](#what-the-dialog-tells-you-about-each-copy).

**A replacement is sent only when it can land on nothing you have.** A file already where the
release would write stops it — even one with the same bytes —, and so does anything the app can't
look at. Nothing reaches qBittorrent then: the dialog says why, and you pick **Keep both** yourself
if you still want the download. See [When a replacement isn't sent](#when-a-replacement-isnt-sent).

## Scheduling a replacement

Find a release on the **Search** page and choose **Download**. Pick the download client and the
destination as always. Under **Existing copies**, **Keep both** is picked, and stays picked unless
you change it; the summary's **Existing copies** then reads "Kept: nothing is replaced or removed,
and a release that would write over a different file isn't sent."

As soon as a destination is picked, the dialog lists **Your copies** of what the release matched in
your library: the film, with every file of every version; for a TV release, the episodes you have of
what its title claims — one episode, a range within one season such as S02E05–E08, or whole seasons
—, by season, each episode with its versions. Each copy shows its file name and folder, its
version's label or "Version 2", the edition its name says (Theatrical, Extended, Director's Cut…),
its resolution and dynamic range as the app measured them, and its size. The line above says which
edition the release's own title names, if any. Nothing is ticked for you.

Pick **Replace after verification** and tick the copies the release should replace. A season has
**Tick every copy that can be replaced** and **Untick all**, and says how many of its copies are
ticked; with several seasons, each is folded until you open it. Switching back to **Keep both**
keeps your ticks, in case you change your mind again.

When the release matched your film only by its title and year — every Toloka release does, as Toloka
lists no ids —, the dialog also asks you to tick **This release is Alien (1979)**, or for a series
**This release is The Penguins of Madagascar (2008), season 2**. **Send** waits for that tick. It
lets the app schedule the replacement, and nothing more: the old copy will move only once Jellyfin
has given the new file that work's own id. When two of your works would read the same — one film in
two libraries, say —, the words also name the library ("… in “Фільми”") or the copy, so you know
which one you confirm. A release matched by its title alone, or whose ids disagree with your film's,
can't replace anything, and the dialog says so.

The summary lists what you ticked, by work — "Alien (1979): 1 copy", "The Penguins of Madagascar,
Season 2: 18 copies" — with each copy's path, then the conditions. **Send** sends the release to
qBittorrent as before, and writes the replacement down with the download first. If a copy changed in
the meantime — another file put in its place, say — **Send** says which and why, reads your copies
again, and keeps the form.

What happens after that: the replacement follows its download — scheduled, submitted,
downloading, downloaded —, and once the download is done the app checks the new file; see [Checking
the new file](#checking-the-new-file). In this version nothing moves after that: a later version
will move the old copy aside once its new file is verified. Until then both copies stay where they
are.

## What the dialog tells you about each copy

A copy you can't tick says why under it:

- **"Its folder isn't ticked for replacement on the Media folders page."** — tick its library folder
  there first; see [Turning replacement on](#turning-replacement-on).
- **"The app can't reach this file …"** — the file has no path in the app, or lies outside the
  **Media folders**.
- **"Not inspected yet"** — the app needs to have read the file once; on the **Library** page,
  **Inspect files** does it.
- **"Changed since the app inspected it."**, **"The file isn't there any more."** or **"The app
  can't read this file now."** — the app looks at each copy again when the dialog reads it, and
  again when you choose **Send**.
- **"The library places this version outside the episodes this release claims."** — a TV copy
  Jellyfin lists under another season or episode than the release's.
- **"A replacement scheduled earlier already holds this copy."** — another download is already set
  to replace it.
- **"This file is also a copy of another work this release matched, which can't be replaced."** —
  the same file, listed under two of your works, one of which the release can't replace.

A copy that can be replaced also says when it has another name — a hardlink: "This file has another
name, so moving it frees no space." The dialog says it from its first read, whether **Keep both** or
**Replace after verification** is picked, as it needs only the disk.

When you pick **Replace after verification**, the app also asks your download clients which torrents
hold your copies, and marks them:

- **Held by a torrent.** "Held by the torrent “Alien.1979.Remastered.1080p” in “qBittorrent”: before
  this copy can move you'll remove that torrent there without its files — which also stops seeding
  its other files." A season pack holding many of your copies is said once, with how many. You can
  still schedule the replacement — the download takes its time, and you can remove the torrent in
  qBittorrent meanwhile. The app never removes, stops or changes a torrent.
- **A torrent's file.** When a copy's other name is a torrent's file, the dialog names that torrent
  too: "It is the torrent “…”'s file."
- **Couldn't check.** When a client couldn't be asked — switched off, out of reach, or a qBittorrent
  older than 5.2, which can't list its torrents' files in one go — the dialog says so: "Couldn't
  check whether a torrent in “qBittorrent” holds these copies: …". The same goes for torrents whose
  files the app couldn't place in its folders. Either way you can still schedule; nothing is taken
  for "not held".

**Replace after verification** can't be picked at all, and the dialog says why, when replacement is
switched off, for a magnet link (its files are unknown until qBittorrent fetches them), for a
destination whose folder isn't inside a library under the **Media folders** or that the app can't
look into, and for a release that matches no film or held episode of yours. Only the owner sees it
enabled: everyone else reads "Only the installation owner can replace a copy." and sends with **Keep
both**, as before.

## When a replacement isn't sent

Right before the release goes to qBittorrent, the app looks at every place its files would land, as
it does for every download. For a replacement it is strict: **any** file already at one of those
places stops it — even one with the same bytes, since a file replaced by itself is no replacement —,
and so does every place it couldn't look at: a link that leads out of the **Media folders**, a file
name it can't look up, a .torrent whose list of files it can't read, or a qBittorrent that couldn't
list the downloads it already has in that category. So does a release whose download link turns out
to be a magnet, and a category whose folder isn't inside a library.

Then nothing reaches qBittorrent. The dialog keeps your choices and says why, such as "“qBittorrent”
would write over “Alien (1979).mkv” in the folder of the category “Movies”: a file is already there,
and a replacement never lands on a file already there, so nothing was sent." The job is listed on
**Jobs** as **Failed** with the same sentence, and its replacement as stopped. The app never sends
it as a plain download on its own: pick **Keep both** and **Send** again if you want it anyway.

## Checking the new file

Within about 15 seconds of qBittorrent finishing the download, the app starts checking it, by
itself, whether replacement is switched on or off. It checks every video file the download brought
— for a season pack, each episode's file on its own — before it touches anything:

1. **Complete.** qBittorrent, asked again, says the download finished without an error and that each
   new file is complete. A pack at 100 % doesn't say on its own which file is done.
2. **Readable.** The app finds the file in its own folders through qBittorrent's path mapping, in
   one of your library folders, and reads it: a regular file, not empty, with a video stream and a
   running time, of the size qBittorrent downloaded, and not one of your old copies. No other
   download in qBittorrent may write the same file.
3. **Seen by Jellyfin.** The app asks Jellyfin to look at the new files — again at its next check,
   within the hour, while Jellyfin doesn't take the request; it asks Jellyfin nothing else —, then
   reads every 15 seconds what Jellyfin made of each, until each is listed, read and identified. A
   trailer or another extra Jellyfin keeps beside the film holds nothing up.
4. **The same film or episode, by Jellyfin's ids.** A film's new file must carry your film's TMDb or
   IMDb id — your own TMDb link counts — and no other id of the same kind. An episode must be of the
   same series, by the series' ids, at the same season and episode number, and Jellyfin must still
   number your old episode, and place it in that series, as when you scheduled it. Each copy you
   ticked pairs with exactly one new file; a new episode no ticked copy pairs with — the extra
   episode a season pack brings, say — is simply added. The other films of a collection simply join
   your library too, once every copy you ticked found its new file.

**Jellyfin gets an hour.** Jellyfin looks at a new file about a minute after it is asked, then reads
and identifies it, which for a new series can take minutes. So the app waits up to an hour from the
moment Jellyfin took its request for each new file to be listed, read and identified, and up to an
hour from the start of the check for qBittorrent to answer. Meanwhile the **Jobs** page says the new
file is being checked. If Jellyfin identified a new file wrongly, or not at all, you can identify it
in Jellyfin yourself within that hour, and the check then passes by itself. A Jellyfin the app can't
reach never lets a file pass.

**When it stops.** If anything is missing or unclear, the replacement stops and says why, and both
copies stay. It stops as soon as it knows:

- qBittorrent reports an error for the download, no longer has it, or hasn't finished one of its
  files — "“qBittorrent” hasn't finished “…” (73 % done)."
- The app can't tell where a new file is — no path mapping covers it, or none of your library
  folders holds it —, or the new file is one of your old copies.
- Another download in qBittorrent writes the same file.
- Jellyfin identified a new file as another film while a copy you ticked has no new file — it may be
  that copy's, identified wrongly —, placed a new episode in another series, or gave a new file ids
  that disagree with yours — "“Living room” identified “…” as another work (IMDb tt0090605), not
  Alien (1979)." — or made a film of an episode, or the other way round.
- Jellyfin now numbers your old episode otherwise than when you scheduled the replacement, or places
  it in another series — "“Living room” now numbers the old copy “…” season 2, episode 3, not season
  2, episode 1 as when it was scheduled."
- A pair is unclear: two new files of one film or episode, none for a copy you ticked, a copy that
  is one half of an episode (its name glues an "a" or a "b" to its S03E09, say), or a file that
  holds two episodes where you ticked one, by Jellyfin's numbers or by its name.
- The new file's name claims another edition than the release did — "“…” claims Extended cut, while
  the release claimed Director's cut."
- The new file runs under three quarters of your old copy's running time, as the app measured it —
  "“…” runs 1:06:40, less than three quarters of the old copy's 1:33:24." A longer cut, an Extended
  or a Redux, is fine, and so is any length when the old copy's was never measured.

Once the hour is up, it stops with what is still missing, such as "“Living room” gave “…” no TMDb or
IMDb id within an hour, so it can't be told to be Alien (1979)." It stops that way, too, when
Jellyfin gave a new file only an id of a kind your film isn't known by in the app, and when
qBittorrent holds a download the app can't place in its folders — a magnet still fetching its list
of files, say —, as it can't tell whether that download writes the new file. A sample file in the
download stops it as well: Jellyfin ignores samples, so it never lists one. These show on the
**Jobs** page as "Replacement needs review: …". A new file that is no usable video — one the app
can't read as media, an empty one, one without a video stream or a running time — stops it as
"Replacement stopped: …".

**Verified, and nothing moved yet.** When every check passes, the line gets a green tick: "New file
verified: 1 copy will be replaced by it in a later version; nothing is moved yet." Both copies stay,
and Jellyfin shows both. A later version moves the old copy aside, checking it again first, and
brings **Retry**, dropping a copy from a replacement and settling an unclear pair; until then a
replacement that needs review stays as it is, both copies kept.

## On the Jobs page

A job that carries a replacement says where it stands, in a line under its status:

- "Replacement scheduled: 1 copy, kept until the new one is verified." — while the download is sent
  and downloads.
- "Replacement checks the new file: 1 copy, kept until it is verified." — once the download is done,
  while its new file is checked.
- "New file verified: 1 copy will be replaced by it in a later version; nothing is moved yet." —
  with a green tick, once every check passed.
- "Replacement needs review: …" with why — the check stopped, or the download couldn't be told apart
  from qBittorrent's other downloads.
- "Replacement stopped: …" with why — the download failed or was cancelled, or a new file is no
  usable video. A failed job says only "Replacement stopped.", as its own error already says why.

While the download goes on, while its new file is checked and once it is verified, the line also
says when replacement has been switched off — nothing will move while it is off — and when a copy's
folder is no longer ticked, as that copy won't move. A replacement that stopped — one that needs
review, failed or was cancelled — goes no further, so its line says none of that; one that needs
review still keeps both copies. The page asks again every 2 seconds while a download goes on or its
new file is checked, so you see how the check ends without reloading. **Cancel job** also ends the
replacement while the download goes on: both copies stay, and the torrent stays in qBittorrent, as
before. A download that is done can't be cancelled there, as before.

## Turning replacement on

Replacement is off after the upgrade, and no folder is ticked. Only the owner can turn it on:

1. Open your user menu → **Media folders**.
2. Under **Folders the app may read**, make sure the folder that holds your libraries is there, as
   the app sees it (`/media`, say).
3. In the **Replacing copies** card below, tick **Replace copies after verification**.
4. Under **Library folders replacement may touch**, tick each library folder whose copies may be
   replaced — `/media/movies` for «Фільми», say. A folder outside the **Media folders** can't be
   ticked. A library folder inside another one needs its own tick: a copy counts only when every
   library folder that holds it is ticked.
5. Choose **Save**. The folders, the switch and the ticks are saved together.

The **Replace after verification** option then appears in the **Download** dialog, for you alone.
For a copy to be ticked there, the app must also have inspected it (the **Library** page's **Inspect
files**, or the inspection after each sync), and the destination's category must save inside one of
your library folders under the **Media folders** — the **Destinations** page says whether it does.
The marks of torrents that hold a copy need qBittorrent 5.2 or later, and so does the check of the
new file: with an older qBittorrent the app can't tell whether another download writes the new file,
so every replacement stops for review there.

Nothing else is needed in this version. Mounting the media folders read-write and running the app as
their owner (`APP_USER_ID` and `APP_GROUP_ID` in `.env`) will be needed only once a later version
moves old copies; scheduling and checking write nothing to the media folders, so keep them mounted
read-only (`:ro`) for now.

Switching replacement off again leaves **Keep both** only in the dialog. Replacements scheduled
before keep following their downloads, and their new files are still checked, as that moves
nothing; the **Jobs** page says that nothing will move while replacement is off.

nothing changed on the setup: the new read of every torrent with its files answered in 355 ms with

needs it.

folders there, and saving **Replace copies after verification** switched on wrote its audit row; the
**Download** dialog of a real Toloka release of a film you own offered **Keep both**, and

## Your files

Scheduling a replacement and checking its new file only read. Once a destination is picked, the app
looks at your copies on the disk — each one's device, inode, size and modification time, the
identity it keeps with the replacement so a later version can find the very same file again —, and
when you pick **Replace after verification** it also reads each download client's list of torrents
with their files. Once the download is done, it reads the new files — their identity, and what the
media inspector finds in them — and asks qBittorrent about the download and its other downloads. It
writes nothing to the media folders: no file is moved, renamed, removed or changed. It writes
nothing to qBittorrent but the one add of the release you sent, as every download does, and never
stops, removes or changes a torrent. It asks Jellyfin to look at the new files' paths — the request
Jellyfin's own folder watcher makes when a file appears — and otherwise only reads Jellyfin: it
never changes, refreshes or identifies an item there. What it keeps — the copies you ticked, the
works they are copies of, your confirmation, who scheduled it and when, and what each check found,
with a row in the app's audit trail for each step — stays in the app's own database.
