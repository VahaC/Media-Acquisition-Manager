## Highlights

**This is the first build of Media Acquisition Manager: it shows the movies you already own beside
the releases you could download, sends the one you pick to qBittorrent, and follows it there until
it is downloaded.** You can open the app in your browser, sign in, see whether its server is ready,
connect your services, import your Jellyfin library, search your library and your release sources
together, choose where downloads go, send a release there and watch its download on the **Jobs**
page. See [What this build does](#what-this-build-does) and
[Coming in later updates](#coming-in-later-updates).

**Everyone signs in with their own account, and the owner decides who has one.** The first time
the app is opened, it asks for the owner account; after that, everyone signs in with a user name and
a password. The owner adds the other people, and can disable an account, reset its password or
delete it — each takes effect at once, on every device that person is signed in on. Everyone
changes their own password on their **Profile** page. See
[Setting up the owner and signing in](#setting-up-the-owner-and-signing-in),
[Managing users](#managing-users) and [Your profile](#your-profile).

**You can connect the app to your services.** Add Jellyfin, a Torznab source such as Prowlarr, and
qBittorrent under **Integrations**, and test each connection before you save it. The app reads
Jellyfin, to import your library, searches your Torznab sources such as Prowlarr, reads
qBittorrent's categories, to choose where downloads go, adds the releases you send to qBittorrent,
and reads how their downloads are getting on. See
[Connecting your services](#connecting-your-services).

**See every movie you own in one list.** The new **Library** page shows the movies of your Jellyfin
library, each with its versions, its files and where the app finds them. Search by title, original
title, IMDb or TMDb id, year or folder, and filter by Jellyfin library or by missing ids. Movies in
collection folders are found where they are: nothing is moved or renamed, and nothing changes in
Jellyfin. The owner imports the library with **Sync now**, and everyone signed in can browse it.
See [Your library](#your-library) and [Importing your library](#importing-your-library).

**See what your files really hold.** The app opens your movie files itself and shows, under each
file, its length, resolution, video format, HDR and bit rate, and its audio tracks and subtitles with
their languages — what is actually in the file, not what its name says. It reads only inside the
media folders the owner allows, which is off until the owner adds one. See
[What's in your files](#whats-in-your-files) and
[Turning on file inspection](#turning-on-file-inspection).

**Choose where downloads go.** The new **Destinations** page names the places your downloads will
go — Movies, Movies 4K — each one a category you already have in qBittorrent. The app never tells
qBittorrent a folder of its own: the category decides, as when you add a torrent yourself, and the
page shows where that is and whether it is inside one of your Jellyfin libraries. See
[Download destinations](#download-destinations) and
[Setting up destinations](#setting-up-destinations).

**Search your library and your release sources at once.** Type a movie's title in the search field
at the top of every page and press Enter: the **Search** page looks for it in your library and in
every release source at the same time. **In your library** shows the copies
you already have, with what their files really hold, then the movies whose title or file path
contains every word you typed, and **Available releases** every release the sources found — even
for a movie you own — with what each one claims to be and which of your copies it is, to filter and
sort as you like. A source that fails says so without hiding the others. See
[Searching](#searching) and [Searching for a movie](#searching-for-a-movie).

**Send the release you pick to qBittorrent.** **Download** on a release opens a dialog where you
pick the download client and the destination — the default destination already picked — and see
what goes where before you choose **Send**. qBittorrent saves the release in that destination's
category folder, just as when you add a torrent yourself, and the dialog tells you whether
qBittorrent took it. A second click or a retry never adds it twice, and nothing you already have is
replaced or removed. Anyone who is signed in can send; the owner sets up qBittorrent and its
destinations first. See [Sending a release to qBittorrent](#sending-a-release-to-qbittorrent) and
[Sending a release](#sending-a-release).

**Follow your downloads until they finish.** The new **Jobs** page lists every release you sent,
newest first, with where it stands in qBittorrent — submitted, downloading, paused, downloaded — how
far it has got, and, when it isn't moving on, why: qBittorrent reports an error for it, no longer
lists it, or can't be asked. Open one to see its files one by one. The app follows your downloads
whether or not the page is open, and goes on after a restart; the page refreshes by itself.
**Cancel job** stops the app following a download, and nothing more: the torrent and its files stay in
qBittorrent. Everyone sees their own jobs only. See
[Following your downloads](#following-your-downloads) and [Cancelling a job](#cancelling-a-job).

**Your media files are left alone.** Nothing in 0.1.0 moves, renames, changes or deletes a file in
your library; a release you send is added beside what you have. See [Your files](#your-files).

## Setting up the owner and signing in

The first time anyone opens the app, it asks them to **create the owner account**: a user name, a
password and the same password again. The owner is the person who adds everyone else and changes
the settings, such as the services the app connects to. Once the owner exists, that page is gone for
good, and everyone gets the sign-in page instead.

- A user name is 3 to 32 characters: letters, digits, dots, hyphens and underscores. Letter case
  doesn't matter when you sign in, so `Olena` and `olena` are the same account.
- A password is at least 8 characters, and every character counts, spaces included — a few words
  make a good password. There is no password reset by email: if the owner forgets their password,
  nobody can reset it from the app, so keep it somewhere safe.

Once signed in, you stay signed in on that browser for 30 days after you last used the app. **Sign
out** is in the menu with your user name, at the top right; signing out in one tab signs out the
app's other tabs in that browser too. If you open a page while signed out, the app asks you to sign
in and then takes you back to that page.

A wrong user name or password only ever gets "The user name or password is incorrect.", never which
of the two was wrong. After 10 attempts in a minute from one address, the app asks you to wait a
minute before trying again. If the server can't be reached, for example while it restarts, the app
says so and tries again every few seconds instead of signing you out.

## Managing users

Only the owner manages users: open the menu with your user name and choose **Users**. Other people
don't have that entry, and opening the page's address anyway only tells them that the owner alone
can do this.

The page lists everyone who can sign in, with when they were added and when they last signed in.
Type in the search box to find someone by part of their user name. A long list is split into pages,
and the number per page you choose is remembered on that device.

- **Add user** — choose their user name and first password, then tell them both.
- **Disable** — they can no longer sign in, and every device they are signed in on is signed out at
  once. **Enable** lets them sign in again; the devices that were signed out stay signed out until
  they sign in anew.
- **Reset password** — sets a new password and signs them out on every device at once; they sign in
  again with the new one. Resetting your own password signs out your other devices and keeps you
  signed in on this one. Once it is set, the page says so above the list.
- **Delete** — asks you to confirm first, then removes the account and signs them out everywhere.
  This can't be undone.

The owner's own account can't be disabled or deleted, so those two buttons are greyed out on its
row. The app records each of these changes — who made it, to whom and when — though there is no
page to read that record yet.

## Your profile

Everyone who is signed in has a **Profile** page: open the menu with your user name, at the top
right, and choose **Profile** — or click your name at the top of the menu. It shows your user name,
whether you are the owner or a member, when your account was created, and when it last signed in,
on this device or another.

**Change password** asks for your current password and then the new one twice; a password is 8 to
128 characters. Changing it signs you out at once on every other device you are signed in on and
keeps you signed in here, and the page says so at its top. After 10 attempts in a minute, the app
asks you to wait a minute before trying again. There is still no password reset by email, so keep
your password somewhere safe.

**Appearance** holds the same **Light**, **Dark** and **System** choice as your user menu, saved in
this browser. **Session** says when this browser will be signed out if you don't use the app again
— 30 days after you last used it — and has **Sign out**.

## Connecting your services

Only the owner connects services: open the menu with your user name and choose **Integrations**.
Other people don't have that entry. The page lists the services you have connected, with their kind
and address. Search by name, or pick a kind to see only media libraries, release sources or download
clients; a long list is split into pages, like the Users page.

**Add integration** asks which service to connect, a name for it, and what that service needs:

- **Jellyfin** — its address, with its base path if it has one (for example
  `http://192.168.1.10:8096`), and an API key: create one in Jellyfin under **Dashboard → API Keys**.
  The app works with Jellyfin 10.9 or later; with an older one, the test names the version it found
  and asks you to update Jellyfin.
- **Torznab** — the feed's Torznab address and its API key. For Prowlarr, that is the indexer's
  address ending in `/1/api` (with the indexer's own number in place of 1), shown in the indexer's
  details, and Prowlarr's own API key from its **Settings → General**.
- **qBittorrent** — the address of its Web UI (for example `http://192.168.1.10:8080`), and its user
  name and password. Leave both empty if qBittorrent lets the app's address in without signing in.
  The app works with qBittorrent 4.4.1 or later; with an older one, the test names the version it
  found and asks you to update qBittorrent.

**Test connection** in the dialog checks what you typed before you save it, and **Test** on a row
checks an integration you saved. Either answers with a sentence saying what happened, such as
"Connected to qBittorrent v5.2.3." or why it couldn't connect. A test saves nothing and changes no
setting of the service. qBittorrent stops accepting sign-ins from an address after several failed
ones, and a test with a wrong password counts as one.

Enter the exact address the service answers at. The app never follows a redirect, so your
credentials can't be sent anywhere else; if a test says the address redirects, use the address it
names instead.

**Path mappings** are for Jellyfin and qBittorrent, which report where your files are. If a service
sees your media folder under another path than the app does, map its folder to the app's: when
qBittorrent sees `/downloads` where the app sees `/media`, add `/downloads` → `/media`. Without
mappings, the app takes the service's paths as they are. The app refuses mappings that would send one
folder to two places. The **Library** page shows the files Jellyfin's mappings don't cover, and the
**Destinations** page the qBittorrent categories its mappings don't cover, so you see at once
whether they are right.

Passwords and API keys are stored encrypted and never shown again, not even to you. When you edit an
integration, their fields are empty: leave them empty to keep what is stored, or type a new one to
replace it. To let the app into qBittorrent without signing in, empty the user name and tick
**Remove the stored password**. If a test says the app can't read a stored password or key — after
its encryption key changed, for example — enter it again and save.

**Use this integration** lets you switch an integration off and keep its settings. A switched-off
Jellyfin can't be synced, but the library it imported stays on the **Library** page, a switched-off
release source isn't searched, and a switched-off qBittorrent isn't offered for sending a release,
and the downloads sent to it aren't followed until it is switched on again — their jobs say so. A
switched-off integration shows **Disabled** and can still be tested. The service can't be changed
once an integration is added: to connect another one, add a new integration.

**Remove** asks you to confirm first, then the app forgets the integration, its credentials and its
path mappings. Removing a Jellyfin also removes the library it imported: its movies, versions and
files leave the **Library** page for everyone, and the confirmation says so. Removing a qBittorrent
also removes the destinations that use it, the default too if it is one of them, and the
confirmation says so as well; their categories stay in qBittorrent. The downloads sent to it are no
longer followed, and their jobs say so until they are cancelled. Nothing changes in the service
itself or in your files.

## Your library

**Library** in the header lists the movies of your Jellyfin library, for everyone who is signed in.
It stays empty until the owner imports the library; see
[Importing your library](#importing-your-library).

Each movie shows:

- its title, its original title when that differs, its year, and the Jellyfin it came from;
- its IMDb and TMDb ids, with a warning badge for each one it lacks. A search finds your movies by
  these ids first, so set a missing one in Jellyfin and sync again;
- its versions, a row each: the version's name, what its file holds at a glance — such as 1080p,
  HEVC, SDR and the languages of its audio tracks, UKR · ENG — or **Not inspected yet**, and its
  size. Choose a row to open it and see its files: the file name, the folder Jellyfin sees it in,
  where this app finds it when that is somewhere else, its size and its Jellyfin library — and, once
  the app has inspected the file, what it really holds (see
  [What's in your files](#whats-in-your-files)). A movie with one version shows it open.

When a movie has two versions or more, choose **Table** under its badges to see them side by side:
a column per version, with the resolution, video, HDR, bit rate, length, audio, subtitles, size and
file of each in rows. **Rows** goes back. Your choice applies to every movie, here and on the
**Search** page, and this browser remembers it. On a phone the versions are always rows.

Anything Jellyfin didn't report — a year, an original title, a size — shows as unknown, never
guessed. Items that Jellyfin keeps apart but that share an IMDb or TMDb id, such as a theatrical cut
and a special edition in one collection folder, are one movie with two versions. Two movies are
never put together because their titles and years match.

Search finds a movie by part of its title or original title in any letter case, by its IMDb or
TMDb id, by its year, or by part of a folder or file name. **Library** narrows the list to the
movies with a file in one Jellyfin library, such as "Фільми", and **Identifiers** to the ones
missing an IMDb or TMDb id. Movies are listed by title in Ukrainian alphabetical order, with titles
in Latin letters after the Cyrillic ones. A long list is split into pages, and the number per page
you choose is remembered on that device.

### Importing your library

Only the owner imports the library:

1. Under **Integrations**, add your Jellyfin with an API key (see
   [Connecting your services](#connecting-your-services)). The app reads libraries from Jellyfin
   10.9 or later.
2. If Jellyfin sees your media folder under another path than the app does, add **Path mappings**
   to that Jellyfin: when Jellyfin sees `/data` where the app sees `/media`, add `/data` → `/media`.
   When both see it at the same path, leave them empty.
3. Open **Library** in the header and choose **Sync now** next to your Jellyfin under **Sources**.

The line under your Jellyfin follows the sync — **Queued…**, then **Syncing… 50 of 870 movies** —
and then says when it finished and what it found; the list fills in by itself. The app doesn't
follow your library on its own: sync again after you add or change movies in Jellyfin.

A sync only reads from Jellyfin, and it reads the whole library before it changes anything here. If
Jellyfin can't be reached, refuses the key, or its library changes while it is being read (during a
library scan, say), the sync fails with a sentence saying why, and the page goes on showing the
library as the last successful sync left it. A sync the app was restarted in the middle of is marked
failed; start it again. Everyone sees that a sync failed, but only the owner sees why, as the reason
can name the server's address.

When some files have no path in this app, a warning under your Jellyfin says how many. The owner
also sees why, and fixes it under **Integrations** → your Jellyfin → **Path mappings**; the files
show their new paths at once, without another sync.

### What's in your files

Once the app has inspected a file, the file shows what is really in it:

- how long it plays, its resolution, its video format (such as HEVC Main 10 or H.264 High), whether
  it is SDR, HDR10, HLG or Dolby Vision, and its overall bit rate;
- its audio tracks, each with its language, format and channels (for example Ukrainian, AC-3, 5.1),
  and which one plays by default;
- its subtitles, each with its language and format, and whether they are forced or for the hard of
  hearing (SDH).

The app reads this from the file with ffprobe, the same tool Jellyfin uses. Whatever the file
doesn't say shows as unknown: a file that doesn't declare its colour range shows "HDR unknown"
rather than a guess, and a track without a language shows "Unknown language". HDR10+ shows as HDR10.
Track names written by whoever made the release, such as the dubbing studio, aren't shown, as they
are a claim rather than something the file proves.

A file the app couldn't read says so instead: it is outside the allowed media folders, missing,
locked, or not a video ffprobe can open. The owner sees the exact reason; everyone else sees a short
one. One unreadable file never stops the others from being inspected.

The app inspects the files after every successful sync, each time it starts, and when the owner
chooses **Inspect files** under **Sources**. The first inspection reads every file and can take a
few minutes on a large library; the line under **Sources** follows it (**Inspecting files… 120 of
870 checked**, and whether another inspection waits after it), and each source says how many of its
files were inspected, couldn't be read or haven't been looked at yet. After that, the app reads a file again only when it changes — another
size, another modification time, or a new file in its place — so later inspections take seconds.
Two names of one file (hardlinks) are read once.

### Turning on file inspection

File inspection is off until the owner tells the app where it may read. Only the owner can do this:

1. Make your media folder visible to the app. With Docker, open `docker-compose.yml`, uncomment the
   media line under `volumes:` and set the left side to the folder on your server, mounting it where
   Jellyfin sees it — for example `- /mnt/media:/media:ro` — then run `docker compose up -d`.
2. Open your user menu → **Media folders**, choose **Add folder**, type the folder as the app sees
   it (`/media` in the example above) and choose **Save**. You can add several.
3. Open **Library** and choose **Inspect files** under **Sources** — or just wait for the next sync.

The app reads only inside the folders you list here. A file outside them, or one reached through a
link that leads out of them, is never opened, even when Jellyfin lists it. If every file says it is
outside the media folders, check that the folder you added is the one the app sees (step 1).

## Download destinations

Only the owner sets up destinations: open the menu with your user name and choose **Destinations**.
Other people don't have that entry.

A destination is a name for where downloads go, such as Movies or Movies 4K, and the qBittorrent
category it stands for. When you send a release, you pick a destination, and qBittorrent saves the
download in that category's folder, just as when you add a torrent in qBittorrent yourself. The app
never gives qBittorrent a folder of its own, and never creates or changes a category.

The page lists your destinations with their download client, their category and where they save: the
folder as qBittorrent sees it, where this app finds it when that is somewhere else, and the Jellyfin
library it is in — "In the media library Фільми". The app works this out by asking qBittorrent each
time you open the page, so a category you change in qBittorrent shows at once. When the folder isn't
in a library, or the app can't tell, the row shows a warning and a sentence saying why:

- **Outside the media libraries** — the folder is in none of your Jellyfin libraries.
- **Folder unknown** — qBittorrent doesn't say which folder the category saves to, or the
  qBittorrent's **Path mappings** don't cover it.
- **Media libraries not known yet** — the app doesn't know your Jellyfin libraries' folders yet:
  sync your library from **Library**. If you have, the sentence asks you to check your Jellyfin's
  **Path mappings** instead.
- **Category missing** — qBittorrent no longer has the category: create it in qBittorrent again, or
  edit the destination and pick another one.
- **Client switched off** — the qBittorrent integration is switched off, so the app doesn't ask it.
- **Client unavailable** — the app couldn't ask qBittorrent: it couldn't be reached, refused the
  password or blocked the app's address (now, or the last time the app signed in: see below), is
  older than 4.4.1, or answered something the app can't read.

A destination with a warning can still be used, but when replacing old copies arrives in a later
version, only a destination inside a library will be able to replace one on its own.

One destination is the **Default**, meant for most of your downloads: the **Download** dialog picks
it first. It is the first destination you add, until you tick **Default destination** on another
one. Unticking it, or removing the default, leaves no default until you pick one.

**Edit** changes a destination's name, client, category or whether it is the default. A new client
or category is checked with qBittorrent first; a new name or the default alone is saved even while
qBittorrent is switched off or away. **Remove** asks you to confirm first and removes the destination
from the app only: the category stays in qBittorrent.

The app signs in to qBittorrent once and keeps that sign-in while its address and password stay the
same; when qBittorrent has forgotten it — after a restart, or an hour unused — the app signs in again
once. If qBittorrent refuses the user name or password, or has blocked the app's address, the app
remembers it and doesn't sign in with them again on its own, since qBittorrent blocks an address
after several failed sign-ins (five in a row, unless you changed it). The page, the dialog and a
save then say so, without asking qBittorrent. Correct the user name or password under
**Integrations**, or, when the stored ones are right again — you set them back in qBittorrent, or
the block has ended — choose **Test** on the qBittorrent row: once qBittorrent takes them, the app
signs in with them again. Restarting the app forgets the refusal too.

### Setting up destinations

Only the owner can do this:

1. Under **Integrations**, add your qBittorrent with its user name and password (see
   [Connecting your services](#connecting-your-services)). The app works with qBittorrent 4.4.1 or
   later.
2. If qBittorrent sees your media folder under another path than the app does, add **Path mappings**
   to that qBittorrent: when qBittorrent sees `/downloads` where the app sees `/media`, add
   `/downloads` → `/media`. A qBittorrent on Windows that saves to a network share shows it the
   Windows way, such as `\\nas\media\Movies`, so map the share: `\\nas\media` → `/media`. With
   qBittorrent 4.4, a folder it lists with forward slashes (`//nas/media/Movies`) shows as **Folder
   unknown**, since on 4.4 that could be a Linux folder too.
3. Open **Library** and choose **Sync now** next to your Jellyfin under **Sources**, so the app knows
   your libraries' folders. If you used the app before this update, sync once more.
4. Open your user menu → **Destinations** and choose **Add destination**. Type a name (such as
   Movies), pick your qBittorrent under **Download client** and one of its categories under
   **Category** — the dialog then shows where that category saves, and whether that is in a library,
   before anything is saved — tick **Default destination** if most downloads should go there, and
   choose **Add destination**.

The app asks qBittorrent whether it has the category before it saves. If it doesn't, or qBittorrent
can't be asked, the dialog says why and nothing is saved. Add one destination for each category you
download into.

## Searching

The search field at the top of every page is for everyone who is signed in: type a movie's title and
press Enter — press / first to jump to the field from anywhere on the page. The **Search** page then
looks for the movie in your library and in every release source the owner has switched on, both at
the same time, and shows what it found in two parts, **In your library** and **Available
releases**. It waits for the slowest source, which can take a few seconds.

**In your library** shows each of your movies that matches, just as the **Library** page shows it —
its versions as rows or side by side in a table, with their files and what they really hold — with a
badge saying how the app recognised it:

- **Matched by IMDb id** or **Matched by TMDb id** — the movie has the id you searched for.
- **Matched by title and year** — one of the movie's titles, the one Jellyfin shows or its original
  title, is the one you searched for, and so is its year.
- **Possible match — check it** — only the title is the same: you gave no year, or the movie is from
  another year. A remake looks like this.
- **Conflicting IMDb ids** (or TMDb ids) — the ids don't agree: two of your Jellyfin libraries give
  the movie different ones, or the movie's isn't the one you searched for. The app shows them all
  and merges nothing, so you can check which is right.

Next to the badge is the title that matched; point at a badge to see which Jellyfin reported the id
or the title. When several of your movies match — the same film in two Jellyfin libraries, or a
film and its remake — all of them are listed, the surest first. Letter case and punctuation don't
matter: "spider man no way home" finds "Spider-Man: No Way Home", and "Oceans Eleven" finds "Ocean's
Eleven". The spelling does, though: the app never matches a title that only looks alike, so "Leon"
doesn't find "Léon", and "and" doesn't find "&".

Below the matches, **In your library** also shows the movies that don't match but whose title, or
the path of one of their files, contains every word you searched for — whole words, in any order,
whatever their year. Search for "rambo", and the movie whose original title is Rambo shows as
**Possible match — check it**; below it come Rambo III, Rambo: Last Blood and the other films of the
series, and even First Blood, whose title doesn't say Rambo but whose file sits in a
`Rambo. Collection` folder. Each of these says it isn't a match: **Title contains “rambo” — not a
match**, or **A file path contains “rambo” — not a match** when only the file's path has the words.
A word counts only whole, so the title "Aliens" doesn't contain "alien"; Aliens shows only when one
of its files' paths does, as one in an `Alien. Collection` folder does. At most 20 such movies are
shown; when more of your movies contain the words, the page says how many and links to the
**Library** page, already searched for what you typed.

**Available releases** lists every release the sources found, even for a movie you already have.
Above the list, each source says how many releases it found, or why it couldn't search — it couldn't
be reached, it refused the key, and so on. A source that fails never hides the others' releases.
Only the owner sees a failure's full reason, as it can name the server's address.

Each release shows its title, its source, its size, its seeders and peers and when it was published,
and under **Claimed** what its title says it is: resolution, format (such as BDRip or WEB-DL), video
codec, HDR, and audio and subtitle languages. These are claims, not facts — the app hasn't seen the
file — and whatever the title doesn't say shows as unknown. When a release is one of the movies
under **In your library**, it says so and names the copy: **In your library: Дюна (2021) — matched by
title and year**, **In your library: … — matched by id**, or **Possible match in your library: …**.
That includes the movies shown because they contain your words: searching for "rambo", a release
"Rambo III (1988) BDRip" says **In your library: Рембо III (1988) — matched by title and year**. A
release is never said to be one of your movies just because its title contains the same words: it
needs that movie's title and year, or its id.

The app tells which movie a release is from its title — "Інтерстеллар / Interstellar (2014) BDRip
1080p" is Інтерстеллар, or Interstellar, from 2014 — and from the IMDb or TMDb id the source lists
with it, when it lists one. Toloka lists none, so its releases are recognised by title and year. A
title without a year, such as "Interstellar BDRip 1080p", doesn't say where the name ends, so such a
release is recognised only by an id. A tag in square brackets at the end of the name isn't part of
it: "Rambo: Last Blood [Extended Cut] (2019)" is Rambo: Last Blood, from 2019, so an extended or
theatrical cut of a movie you have says **In your library** too. Square brackets at the start or in
the middle of a name stay, as in "[REC] 2", and round brackets always do.

Each release has three buttons:

- **Details** — everything the source listed, what the app read from the title and the ids in the
  listing, and beside them the copies in your library it may be, with what their files really hold,
  so you can compare.
- **Open source** — the release's page at the source, in a new tab. Greyed out when the source gave
  no page.
- **Download** — sends the release to qBittorrent, into the destination you pick. See
  [Sending a release to qBittorrent](#sending-a-release-to-qbittorrent).

A search is kept for a day. Filtering, sorting, going to another page and reloading read what the
search found and don't ask the sources again, and the page's address keeps the search, so reloading
or going back brings it back. To see what is new at a source, search again. After a day the search
is gone, and the page says "This search is no longer available. Search again." Your searches are
your own: nobody else can open them, not even the owner.

### Searching for a movie

1. Type the title in the search field at the top of the page — the one Jellyfin shows or the
   original one, in any letter case — and press Enter. Press / on any page to jump to the field.
   For a surer match, add the year in brackets at the end: `Interstellar (2014)`. A number without
   brackets stays part of the title, as in Blade Runner 2049.
2. The page says **Results for “Interstellar”.** and **Searching…** until every source has
   answered. If the search can't be made, the reason shows in a banner under the form; **Dismiss**
   clears it.
3. To narrow the results, fill in the **Year**, or the movie's **IMDb id** (such as `tt0816692`) or
   **TMDb id** (such as `157336`), under them and choose **Apply**: an id finds your copy whatever
   its title, and goes to the sources that can search by it. Each is optional. **Apply** makes a new
   search with the same title, and Back returns to the one before.
4. Under **In your library**, see the copies you already have and how sure each match is, and
   below them the movies that only contain the words you typed.
5. Under **Available releases**, narrow the list with **Source**, **Audio language**, **Quality** —
   each of the last two with **Unknown** for releases whose title names none — and **Size, GiB**,
   **From** and **To** (such as 4.5), and order it with **Sort**: **Most seeders**, **Largest**,
   **Smallest**, **Newest** or **Best quality**. If no release is left, **Clear filters** shows them
   all again.
6. Choose **Details** on a release to compare it with your copies, or **Open source** to read its
   page at the source.
7. To get a release, choose **Download** on it; see [Sending a release](#sending-a-release).

## Sending a release to qBittorrent

**Download** on a release under **Available releases** sends it to qBittorrent, into one of your
destinations. Anyone who is signed in can send a release from their own search, for as long as the
search is kept — a day. The owner sets up where releases can go; see
[Setting up sending](#setting-up-sending).

The dialog offers only the download clients that can take the release, and under **Destination**
the destinations of the client you pick. It opens with the default destination picked; when there is
no default, with the only client picked, and its destination if it has just one; otherwise you pick.
Its **Summary** shows what goes where: the source, the release, the download client, the destination
with its qBittorrent category, and **Existing copies — Kept**: this version of the app never replaces
or removes a file, so the release is added beside any copy you already have.

When you choose **Send**, the app fetches the release from its source itself and hands it to
qBittorrent with the destination's category, and qBittorrent saves it in that category's folder —
also when qBittorrent adds new torrents in manual mode, as the app asks for automatic torrent
management on the torrent it adds. The release's download link, and the tracker addresses a magnet
link carries, never reach your browser. First, though, the app checks that qBittorrent still has the
category, and that it doesn't already have this torrent.

Once qBittorrent has answered, the dialog says what happened:

- **qBittorrent took the release and lists it among its downloads.** It is downloading, or waiting
  its turn, in qBittorrent.
- **qBittorrent took the release, but hasn't listed it among its downloads yet.** qBittorrent said
  yes but hadn't shown it in its list moments later. It usually appears there shortly after.
- **qBittorrent took the release, but the app can't tell which of its downloads it is, so it has to
  be linked by hand.** qBittorrent took it, but lists more than one download it could be, or the
  release didn't say which torrent it is. It downloads all the same; linking it in the app comes in
  a later update. Until then its job says **Needs linking**, and the app doesn't follow it; to send
  the release again, cancel that job first (see [Cancelling a job](#cancelling-a-job)).
- **A sentence saying why it wasn't sent**, above the buttons: qBittorrent couldn't be reached or
  refused the app, no longer has the category, already has this torrent, or the source couldn't give
  the release. Nothing new reached qBittorrent. The dialog keeps your choices, so you can fix the
  cause, pick again and choose **Send** for a new try. If you aren't the owner and qBittorrent or the
  source failed, the sentence names it and asks you to have the owner check it under **Integrations**:
  what a service says can name its address, which is the owner's.
- **This release is already being sent to qBittorrent. Its outcome isn't known yet.** You sent it
  again while the first try was still on its way; the first try decides.

A second click on **Send**, or **Send** again after the app lost its connection, never adds the
release twice: the app answers a repeat with what the first one did. A release already sent to that
qBittorrent — by you or anyone else, from this search or another — is refused with a sentence, unless
that try failed, or its job was cancelled. A torrent qBittorrent already has, added there by hand, is
not sent either: the app never takes over a download it didn't add. The app records each release
sent — who sent it, which release, to which client and destination, and when — and you find each of
yours on the **Jobs** page; see [Following your downloads](#following-your-downloads).

### Setting up sending

Nothing needs turning on beyond a download client and a destination. Only the owner can set them up:

1. Under **Integrations**, add your qBittorrent (4.4.1 or later) and keep **Use this integration**
   on; see [Connecting your services](#connecting-your-services).
2. Under **Destinations**, add at least one destination for it, and tick **Default destination** on
   the one most downloads should go to; see [Setting up destinations](#setting-up-destinations).

The releases of a switched-off source can't be sent, as the app can't fetch them; switch the source
on under **Integrations**.

### Sending a release

1. Search for the movie; see [Searching for a movie](#searching-for-a-movie).
2. Under **Available releases**, choose **Download** on the release you want.
3. Under **Download client**, pick your qBittorrent, and under **Destination**, where the release
   should go. The default destination is already picked; pick another if you like.
4. Read the **Summary**: the release, the download client, the destination with its category, and
   that existing copies are kept.
5. Choose **Send**. The button says **Sending…** until qBittorrent has answered, then the dialog says
   what happened (see above). Choose **Close**.
6. To see how the download is getting on, open **Jobs** in the header; see
   [Following your downloads](#following-your-downloads).

## Following your downloads

**Jobs** in the header lists the releases you sent, newest first. Everyone sees their own jobs only —
the owner too. Each row shows the release, which opens its job; its status; how far the download has
got, or **Not known yet** before qBittorrent has said; where it went — the download client and the
destination; and when you sent it. The list above it, **All statuses** until you pick one, narrows it
to the jobs of one status; when none of yours has it, **Clear filters** shows them all again. On a
phone each job is a card.

The app reads qBittorrent every 2 seconds, whether or not the page is open, so a download is followed
while you are away and again after the app restarts. While the page shows a download still under way,
it refreshes by itself as often; once every job on it has ended, it stops asking.

### What a job's status means

- **Sending** — the app is sending the release to qBittorrent. If the app was stopped in the middle of
  a send, the job stays **Sending** for five minutes; then the app looks for the release in
  qBittorrent and follows it if qBittorrent has it, or marks the job **Failed** with the reason.
- **Submitted** — qBittorrent took the release, and hasn't started downloading it yet.
- **Downloading** — qBittorrent is downloading it, or it is waiting or being checked again after it
  started.
- **Paused** — the download is paused in qBittorrent. It moves on once you resume it there.
- **Downloaded** — qBittorrent has the whole download. The app stops following it here.
- **Failed** — the release wasn't sent, or the app couldn't tell whether it was; the reason shows in
  red under the status.
- **Needs linking** — qBittorrent took it, but the app can't tell which of its downloads it is, so it
  doesn't follow it.
- **Cancelled** — you stopped the app following it; see [Cancelling a job](#cancelling-a-job).

### Why a download isn't moving on

When the app can't move a job on, a line under its status says why. The job keeps its status, the app
keeps following it, and the line goes by itself once the cause does:

- **qBittorrent reports an error for this download** — a disk that is full or missing, or files that
  went missing. Check it in qBittorrent; the job goes on once the error clears.
- **qBittorrent doesn't list this download any more** — it was removed in qBittorrent. The app keeps
  following it in case the same torrent comes back. To send the release again, cancel the job first.
- **qBittorrent took this download but hasn't listed it since** — for more than a minute after it
  took it.
- **qBittorrent can't be asked** — it was removed or switched off under **Integrations**, couldn't be
  reached, or refused the app's user name or password. If you aren't the owner, the line names
  qBittorrent and asks you to have the owner check it under **Integrations**. Once qBittorrent has
  refused the password, the app doesn't try it again on its own, as each refused sign-in counts
  towards qBittorrent blocking the app: the jobs move on again once the owner corrects it under
  **Integrations**, or chooses **Test** there when qBittorrent takes it again.

### A job's details

Choose a release on the **Jobs** page to open its job: its source, the download client, the
destination with its qBittorrent category, its status and progress, when you sent it and when its
status last changed, and qBittorrent's id for the download. Under **Files** are its files as
qBittorrent last listed them, in qBittorrent's order, each with its size and progress; **Skipped**
marks a file qBittorrent was told not to download. A magnet link lists its files only once
qBittorrent has fetched what the torrent holds. **Jobs** takes you back to the list.

### Cancelling a job

**Cancel job** stops the app following a download, and does nothing else: the app sends nothing to
qBittorrent, so the torrent and its files stay there, downloading or seeding as before. To stop or
remove the download itself, do it in qBittorrent. Only a job the app still follows, or one that needs
linking, can be cancelled. A job just sent and still **Created** may still be on its way to
qBittorrent, so it offers no **Cancel job** for its first 5 minutes.

1. On the **Jobs** page or the job's own page, choose **Cancel job**.
2. Read the confirmation: the torrent and its files stay in qBittorrent, and nothing is paused,
   stopped or removed there.
3. Choose **Cancel job**, or **Keep following** to leave it as it is.

The job then says **Cancelled**, and the app never asks qBittorrent about it again; this can't be
undone. A job that finished or failed in the meantime isn't cancelled, and a line says so. Once a job
is cancelled, its release can be sent again from Search — though while qBittorrent still has the
torrent, the app refuses it as one qBittorrent already has.

## When something goes wrong

Whatever fails, the app says what it was, with the server's own reason when it gives one, and leaves
what you typed or had on screen where it was.

- **A page or a list that can't load** shows a box in its place that says what failed and why, with
  **Try again**. It reads **Retrying…** while it asks. When asking again can't help — a search that
  has expired, a job that isn't yours, a page only the owner may open — there is no button.
- **A list that can't refresh** keeps what it showed, under a line that says so, with **Retry**.
- **An action that is refused**, such as **Remove**, **Disable** or **Cancel job**, says why above
  the list; **Dismiss** clears it. So does the answer of a connection **Test**. A refused **Sync
  now** or **Inspect files** says so on its own row.
- **A form or a dialog that can't save** says why above its buttons and keeps everything you typed.
  A field you got wrong, such as two passwords that differ or a user name that is already taken, is
  marked red with the reason under it.
- **The server can't be reached** — for example while it restarts — a strip under the header says
  so. The app asks again every few seconds, or at once with **Retry now**, and the strip goes as
  soon as the server answers; whatever failed meanwhile is asked for again by itself.

## Ready for other languages

The app is in English only for now. All of its text is kept apart from the code, so a translation
can be added later without changing the app itself. There is no language setting yet: it arrives
with the second language. Messages that come from the server, such as the reason a status check
failed, stay in English.

## Running it with Docker

The app comes as a ready-made Docker image. Put the project's `docker-compose.yml` and `deploy.sh`
in an empty folder, run `docker compose up -d` there, and open <http://localhost:8118>. There is
nothing to set up first: on its first start the app creates its database and its keys by itself and
keeps them in a Docker volume, so updating the app keeps them. The first page asks you to create the
owner account; see [Setting up the owner and signing in](#setting-up-the-owner-and-signing-in). To
update later, run `bash deploy.sh` in the same folder.

### Turning on access from other devices

The app opens only on the computer it runs on until you choose otherwise. To open it from your other
devices:

1. **Create the owner account first**, in a browser on the computer the app runs on
   (<http://localhost:8118>). Until the owner exists, whoever opens the app first can make
   themselves the owner.
2. In the folder with `docker-compose.yml`, create a file named `.env` (you can start from a copy of
   the project's `.env.example`).
3. Add the line `APP_BIND_ADDRESS=0.0.0.0` to it.
4. Run `docker compose up -d` again.

Everyone who can reach that computer then gets the sign-in page. The app talks plain HTTP, which
isn't encrypted: passwords and everything else cross the network as they are. Open it only on a
network you trust, or reach it through a reverse proxy that adds HTTPS. Behind such a proxy the app
sees everyone as coming from the proxy, so the limit of 10 sign-in attempts a minute counts for all
visitors together: someone failing on purpose can keep everyone else from signing in for as long as
they carry on.

## Tested with

This build was tried on a real home setup, with these versions:

- **Jellyfin 12.1.0** — importing a library of about 870 movies, collection folders included,
  finding a movie inside a collection folder from the search where it is, and nothing in the library
  moved, changed or removed afterwards.
- **Prowlarr 2.6.5 with the Toloka indexer** — searches, which list Toloka's releases beside your
  copies, and a source that fails: **Available releases** says what went wrong with it, and your
  library is still shown.
- **qBittorrent 5.2.4** — its categories as destinations, a release added into the Movies
  category's folder, and a second **Send** of the same release refused, with no second download.

Not tried on a real setup yet:

- Jackett, indexers other than Toloka, and sources that give magnet links.
- qBittorrent older than 5.2, or running on Windows.
- Jellyfin older than 12.1.
- A download followed on **Jobs** all the way to **Downloaded**, and a job's files.
- **Inspect files** on a real media folder.

## Your files

The app itself never writes to your media folders. Importing your library only reads from Jellyfin,
and moves, renames or changes nothing, in your folders or in Jellyfin. Inspecting your files only
reads them, and only inside the media folders the owner allowed. Destinations only read from
qBittorrent — its version, its categories and its default download folder — and add, change and
remove nothing there. Sending a release adds one torrent to qBittorrent, in the destination's
category only, and qBittorrent downloads it into that category's folder, beside what is already
there; the app never moves, replaces or removes a file, and never changes or removes a torrent in
qBittorrent. Following your downloads only reads from qBittorrent, every 2 seconds, and **Cancel
job** sends it nothing: the torrent and its files stay as they are. Searching only reads your
library as the app imported it and asks your release sources, and changes nothing in either. The
only things the app writes are its own: a small database, which also holds what it imported from
your library, what it read from your files, your destinations, your searches with the releases they
found, each kept for a day, and the downloads you sent, with the releases they sent and what
qBittorrent last said of each and its files, and the keys it generates on first start, both kept in
its data folder. In
Docker, the example media folders in `docker-compose.yml` are mounted read-only, so the app couldn't
change them even by mistake.
