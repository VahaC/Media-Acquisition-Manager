## Highlights

**Your series are in the app now, beside your movies.** The **Library** page shows each series from
Jellyfin as one card with its seasons; open a season to see its episodes, each with its versions and
files. A show Jellyfin splits into several series, one per season folder, is still one card, and an
episode that belongs to no series has a card of its own. See
[Series in your library](#series-in-your-library).

**Search for a series and download its releases.** The **Search** page now finds TV releases as well
as movies, matches them to the series you have, and tells you how many episodes of the seasons a
release offers you already hold. Add a season to look for that season alone. See
[Searching for a series](#searching-for-a-series).

**Downloads go where their tracker section says.** Tell a destination which sections of your release
source belong there — «Мультсеріали» to Cartoons, «Телесеріали» to TV Shows — and the **Download**
dialog picks it for every release from those sections. See
[Choosing the destination by section](#choosing-the-destination-by-section).

**The app looks through your media folders itself.** **Scan folders** on the **Library** page goes
through every media folder the owner allowed and finds every video file there, and the ones
Jellyfin doesn't list among them; the app then reads those files as it reads your library's. It only
reads, and changes nothing. See [Scanning your media folders](#scanning-your-media-folders).

**The owner turns them on.** Series arrive with the next library sync, the media folders are
scanned when the owner chooses **Scan folders**, and a destination picks releases by section once
the owner ticks its sections. See [Turning it on](#turning-it-on).

**Still to come in this update: finding duplicates** among your movie and episode files, with the
reasons. See [Coming in this update](#coming-in-this-update).

## Series in your library

The **Library** page lists your movies and your series together, in title order. A series card says
**Series** and shows the show's titles, its IMDb, TMDb TV and TVDB ids with a warning for each one it
lacks, and its seasons: **Season 1**, **Season 2** and on, then **Specials**, then **No season** for
the episodes Jellyfin gives no season, each with how many episodes it holds. The seasons come from
the episodes you have, so a season you hold no episode of isn't listed.

Choose a season to open it. Its episodes load, each with its number and title, the ids it lacks, and
its versions and files as rows, just like a movie's. A long season is shown a page at a time, and
you can open several seasons at once.

An episode Jellyfin puts in no series has a card of its own that says where it sits, such as
"Season 1 · Episode 4". Episodes Jellyfin only knows of from the show's details, with no file —
mostly specials — are not shown: the page lists what you hold.

**Kind** narrows the list to **Movies** or **Series**. The search, the library filter and **Missing
an id** reach into the episodes: a series is listed with the episodes that match, or with all of
them when the series itself matches. **Missing an id** means an id of the work's own kind: IMDb and
TMDb for a movie, IMDb, TMDb TV and TVDB for a series, IMDb and TVDB for an episode.

A show Jellyfin knows as several series, all with the same ids, is one card. A movie and a series
are never mixed up, even when the movie database gives them the same number. A sync now reads your
movies, your series and your episodes, and its line says how many items it read and how many works
and series it stored.

## Searching for a series

Type the series' title in the search field at the top and press Enter, as for a movie. The
**Search** page asks every release source for movies and then for TV, in the source's TV sections,
so a search takes a little longer than before.

- **In your library** shows a series you have as its card with its seasons. An episode whose title,
  id or file path matches what you typed shows its series.
- **Available releases** lists the TV releases beside the movies. A TV release shows **TV** among
  what it claims, with the seasons ("Seasons 1–5") or episodes ("Episodes 1–9") its title names, and,
  when it matches one of your series, how many episodes of those seasons you already have — "None of
  its episodes in your library" when you have none. **Details** shows the span of years and the
  seasons read from the title.
- **Kind** narrows the list to **Movies** or **TV**.

A TV release is only ever matched to a series, and a movie release to a movie: a film and a show
with the same title never claim each other's releases. Toloka lists no ids, so its TV releases match
by title and year. A release names the years of the seasons it holds, not the year the show began,
so how its year is read depends on those seasons:

- **With season 1**, its first year must be the show's: "Yellowstone (S1-5) (2018-2024)" matches
  your Yellowstone (2018) by title and year, while a documentary "Yellowstone (S1) (2009)" matches
  it by title alone, as a possible match to check.
- **With later seasons only**, the show began in that first year or before: "Yellowstone (S5)
  (2022-2024)" and "(S4) (2021)" match Yellowstone (2018) by title and year too. If you have an
  older show with the same title as well, such a release matches both.
- **With no season**, any year of its span, such as "(2018-2024)", matches.

To look for one season:

1. Search for the series by its title.
2. Under the search, fill in **Season** — 1, or 0 for the specials — and choose **Apply**.
3. Only TV releases are searched then, and a source that can search by season is asked for that
   season. Toloka narrows little by season, so expect other seasons among the results, each with the
   seasons it claims.

**Download** sends a TV release as it sends a movie; see
[Choosing the destination by section](#choosing-the-destination-by-section).

## Choosing the destination by section

Release sources file their releases in sections: Toloka has «Мультсеріали», «Телесеріали», several
«Документальні фільми» sections and many more, beside standard categories such as TV and TV/HD. The
owner can tell each destination which sections go there. **Download** on a release then opens the
dialog on the destination its section goes to: a release in «Мультсеріали» opens on Cartoons. The
source's own section counts before a standard category.

When none of the release's sections is ticked for a destination, the dialog opens on the default
destination, as before. When two of the source's own sections of the release are ticked for
different destinations — or, with none of its own ticked, two standard categories — it opens with
none picked, for you to choose. You can always pick another destination before you send.

## Scanning your media folders

Jellyfin lists the files it made movies and episodes of. Your media folders can hold more: the
clips of a Blu-ray or DVD folder, files Jellyfin hasn't picked up yet, files in a folder no library
covers. The app now finds them itself.

Under **Sources** on the **Library** page, after your Jellyfin, the **Media folders** row stands for
the media folders the owner allowed. **Scan folders** goes through all of them, every folder inside
included, hidden ones too, and lists each video file it finds: a file whose name ends in `.mkv`,
`.mp4`, `.avi`, `.m2ts`, `.iso` or another video extension. It only reads the names in your folders —
it opens no file and changes nothing. It then compares each file with what your libraries list:

- **A file Jellyfin lists, a movie's or an episode's, is recognised** by its path — where the app
  sees it, through the path mappings — so it is already in your library here and nothing more is
  kept of it. Two files are never taken for one because their names look alike.
- **A file no library lists is remembered** by its path. Once the scan is done, the app inspects it
  as it inspects your library's files, so it is read once and again only when it changes.

The row tells you how it went:

- **Scanning the media folders… 120 files found** while it works.
- **Scanned** with the time, how many video files it found and how many of them are in no library.
  A file also reached through a link counts once.
- **Files in no library**: how many of those the app has inspected, couldn't read, or hasn't
  inspected yet.
- How many folders or links it didn't go into, when there were some. The owner also sees the first
  one's reason; everyone else is asked to check with the owner.

A link inside your media folders — or a junction on Windows — is followed, and the files behind it
are listed under the link's own name. A link that leads out of the media folders is not followed,
and neither is one that leads back to a folder above it. A folder the app can't open right now — a
disk that isn't mounted, a folder it isn't allowed to read — is counted as not scanned, and what the
app found there before is kept. So is a media folder that is completely empty, as it may be a disk
that is away.

Everyone sees the row; only the owner can start a scan. The app scans only when the owner chooses
**Scan folders** — never on a timer, after a sync or when it starts — so scan again after you add
files that Jellyfin doesn't list. The row shows how many files are in no library, not which ones,
and the app doesn't guess from a file's name which movie or episode it is. Finding the duplicates
among all your files is still to come; see [Coming in this update](#coming-in-this-update).

## Turning it on

Only the owner can do this.

**Your series.** After this update, open **Library** and choose **Sync now** next to your Jellyfin
under **Sources**. Until then the app shows your movies alone.

**Your media folders.** Nothing is scanned until the owner asks.

1. If no media folder is allowed yet, open your user menu → **Media folders**, choose **Add
   folder**, type the folder as the app sees it — `/media` with the example Docker setup — and
   choose **Save**. Until then the **Media folders** row says scanning is off, with a link there.
2. Open **Library** and choose **Scan folders** on the **Media folders** row under **Sources**.
3. The row follows the scan, and the inspection of the files it found starts on its own once it is
   done. The first one reads every new file, which can take a while on a large disk.

**Destinations by section.** Until a section is ticked, the **Download** dialog opens on the default
destination, as before.

1. Open your user menu → **Destinations**, and choose **Edit** on a destination, or **Add
   destination** for a new one.
2. Under **Tracker sections (optional)**, open your release source, such as Toloka through Prowlarr.
   The app asks it for its sections: its own first, then the standard categories.
3. Tick the sections whose releases should go to this destination — for example «Мультсеріали» and
   «Мультфільми» for Cartoons, «Телесеріали» for TV Shows, the «Документальні фільми» sections for
   Documentary. A section already ticked for another destination says so, such as "now Cartoons";
   ticking it here moves it.
4. Choose **Save changes**, or **Add destination** for a new one. Do the same for each destination.

A source that can't be asked — switched off, or not answering — says why, and still shows the
sections already ticked for this destination, so saving keeps them.

## Your files

Everything this update adds only reads your library, and a release you send is added beside what you
have. Scanning your media folders only reads the names in them, only inside the media folders the
owner allowed, and opens, moves, renames or removes no file; the inspection after it only reads
files, as before. The app goes by your library as it is: a file Jellyfin lists as a movie stays a
movie, even when its name says it is an episode.
