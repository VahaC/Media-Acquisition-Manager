## Highlights

**Find what your library is missing.** The new **Recommendations** page suggests movies, series,
cartoons and documentaries you don't have yet, picked from what you do have: the next film of a
collection you own part of, more from a director whose films you keep, what goes with the works in
your library, the seasons of your series you don't have. Every suggestion says why it is there, and
you can sort a list by rating, date or what Toloka has. See
[What the page shows](#what-the-page-shows).

**See what Toloka has of each.** A card tells you how many releases Toloka has of the work, the best
quality and whether there is Ukrainian audio. The app checks a few works at a time, within a daily
limit you set yourself. See
[Releases on Toloka and your daily limit](#releases-on-toloka-and-your-daily-limit).

**New on Toloka.** Every few hours the app reads Toloka's newest releases and suggests the ones you
don't have yet. See [New on Toloka](#new-on-toloka).

**Hide what you don't want.** **Not interested** takes a work off your lists, and only yours. See
[Not interested](#not-interested).

**Choose your lists.** In your **Profile**, switch each of the four lists on or off and set how many
searches the app may run for you a day. See [Your Profile settings](#your-profile-settings).

**Nothing is downloaded by itself.** **Releases** on a card opens the **Search** page for that work;
you pick a release and send it with the **Download** dialog, as always.

**Link your cartoons and documentaries to TMDb.** Many works in your library have no TMDb id, so the
app can't tell which film or show they are. The owner now picks it — from suggestions the app
prepares, from a link you paste, or from a TMDb search. For a film, the app writes that id into its
item in Jellyfin, and nothing else; a series' link is kept in the app. See
[Linking a work to TMDb](#linking-a-work-to-tmdb).

**One at a time, or every sure one at once.** The new **TMDb links** page walks through the works
without an id with the keyboard, and links every confident suggestion with one click. See
[The TMDb links page](#the-tmdb-links-page).

**The owner turns both on** by connecting TMDb, a free film and TV catalog; only the owner links
works. See [Turning it on](#turning-it-on).

### Also in this release

- **Anime and documentary films are found as films** — and Toloka's own title shows under each
  release. See [Changes to the search](#changes-to-the-search).
- **A wider page on a big screen** — up to 1280px instead of 1024px. See
  [A wider page](#a-wider-page).

## What the page shows

Choose **Recommendations** in the header. It has four lists, **Movies**, **Series**, **Cartoons** and
**Documentaries**: a documentary is in **Documentaries**, even when it is animated; an animation,
anime included, is in **Cartoons**; anything else is in **Movies** or **Series**. **Cartoons** and
**Documentaries** hold films and series both, and a filter shows one or the other.

Each card shows the poster, the title in Ukrainian and in the original, the year, the genres and the
rating with how many people voted, then why the work is there:

- **"Guardians of the Galaxy · 2 of 3"** — a collection you have part of: the films of it you don't
  have.
- **"Ridley Scott · 12 in your library"** — a director with two or more films in your library, or the
  creator of two or more of your series: their other works.
- **"Because you have Alien, Aliens and 3 more"** — the film and TV catalog recommends it beside works
  you have. The more of your works it goes with, the higher it is.
- **"Missing seasons 4–5"** — a series you have, with seasons that have aired and of which you have no
  episode. Specials don't count, and a season you have even one episode of isn't missing.
- **"New on Toloka · 2 days ago"** — Toloka has just published it; see [New on Toloka](#new-on-toloka).
- **"Top rated"** — for a list your library has few works of, highly rated works, shown only once
  Toloka has releases of them.

The order is the same every time, and it follows from those reasons and the rating: missing seasons
of your series count most, then the rest of your collections, then directors and creators you like.
A work goes higher the more of your works point at it, the better it is rated by many people, when
it is new on Toloka, and when it is like the rest of your library in genre and decade. A work
recommended only because it is like what you have needs a good rating from enough people; the next
film of your collection, your series' missing seasons and what is new on Toloka are shown whatever
their rating. A work that isn't out yet is never shown, and neither is one you already have — by its
ids, or by its title and year — or one a file in your media folders is named after.

Filter a list by **Reason**, **Genre** or **Decade**, by **Films or series** in **Cartoons** and
**Documentaries**, and tick **Available only** for the works Toloka has releases of. **Sort** puts
the list in another order: **Best match** (the order above), **Rating** — weighed by how many people
voted, so a 10 from three votes doesn't come first —, **Most votes**, **Newest**, **Oldest**,
**Title A–Z** or **Most releases** on Toloka. Works of the same rank keep the best-match order, and
the page's address keeps your choice, so a link or a reload opens the list sorted the same way.

**Releases** on a card opens the **Search** page for the work, with its title and year. Pick a
release there and send it with **Download**; the dialog picks the destination by the release's
section, as before, and the download appears under **Jobs**. While anyone's download of the work is
under way, its card says **Downloading**; once that download has finished — or was cancelled, or
waits to be linked — the card leaves everyone's lists. A download that failed changes nothing.

The lists are computed again after every library sync. The line above them says when they last
were, and the owner can choose **Recompute now**. While the lists are being computed, it says what
the app is doing — reading your library, reading the works from the catalog, and so on —, how far it
is, such as "240 of 815", with a progress bar, and how long ago it started: the first time takes a
few minutes. Film and show data comes from TMDB.

## Releases on Toloka and your daily limit

Under each card's reasons, a line says what Toloka has of the work:

- **"4 releases · up to 2160p · Ukr"** — how many releases match the work, the best quality they
  claim, and **Ukr** when one has Ukrainian audio.
- **"Not found (checked 3 days ago)"** — Toloka had nothing for it then.
- **"Not checked yet"** — the app hasn't asked yet.

The app asks Toloka by itself, a little at a time: at most 4 works every 15 minutes for everyone
together, users taking turns, and for your lists at most your **daily search limit** in any 24 hours
— 60 unless you change it. A search counts even when Toloka doesn't answer. Your limit is shared
equally between your lists that are on, and a list with nothing left to check gives its share to the
others. Works never checked go first, then the ones at the top of your lists. What Toloka had is
shared by everyone: a work checked for someone else isn't searched again for you. A work Toloka has
is checked again after a day, one it didn't have after a week. Works you marked **Not interested**,
and those of a list you switched off, are never searched for you. So the lists fill in over a few
days, not at once.

The app searches Toloka by the work's original title when it is written in Latin letters, and by its
Ukrainian title otherwise; a film with its year. A release counts only when it is that work — the
same film or series, by its title and year — and for a series you have, only when it holds a season
you are missing.

These checks only look. **Releases** on the card asks Toloka again, on the **Search** page, so you
always pick from what it has now.

## New on Toloka

Every 3 hours, and when the app starts, it reads Toloka's newest releases — the latest 90 in the
sections your lists cover — and looks each one up in the film and TV catalog by Toloka's own title
and year. A work you don't have is recommended with **"New on Toloka · 2 days ago"** for two weeks
after the app first saw it. Reading the newest releases doesn't count against anyone's daily limit.

## Not interested

**Not interested** on a card hides the work from your lists, and says **Hidden** with **Undo** in its
place in case you didn't mean it. It is your choice alone: everyone else still sees the work. It stays
hidden when the lists are computed again, and the app never searches Toloka for it on your behalf.

## Your Profile settings

Open your user menu → **Profile**. The **Recommendations** card has:

- **Movies**, **Series**, **Cartoons** and **Documentaries** — the lists you see. All four are on
  until you change them. A list you switch off is hidden, and its works are never searched for you;
  with all four off, **Recommendations** leaves the header.
- **Daily search limit** — how many Toloka searches the app may run for your lists in 24 hours, a whole
  number from 0 to 500. 0 runs none: your cards then say "Not checked yet" unless a search for someone
  else checked them.
- How many searches the app ran for you in the last 24 hours.

Choose **Save**. Switching on a list nobody else has on computes the lists again, so its works
appear after a few moments. Only you see and change your settings.

## Linking a work to TMDb

Only the owner links works; everyone else sees the button disabled. On the **Library** page, choose
**TMDb…** on a work's card. The dialog shows:

- **This work** — the ids Jellyfin reports for each of the work's items, and the work's link, with
  who set it and when.
- **Suggestions** — up to five TMDb films and shows, the likeliest first, each with its poster, title,
  year, runtime and overview, and why it is suggested: "Same title", "Title 80% alike", "Same year"
  or "1 year apart", "6 min ≈ 6 min" (how long the app measured the file to be against TMDb's
  runtime), "A film" or "A show: the work links as its episode", "Animation, as its library", "Same
  director as …" (a work beside it in the same folder that you linked already). **Confident** marks
  the one suggestion whose kind, title, year and runtime all agree with the work.
- **Paste a link** — a TMDb or IMDb link, an IMDb id like tt0056164, or a TMDb number (then say
  whether it is a **Film** or a **Show**), and **Look up**.
- **Search TMDb** — a title and, if you like, a year, and **Search**. It starts from the work's title
  without what a file name adds — "Landing Stripling" of "120. Landing Stripling" — and its year.

Pick one and choose **Save**. When Jellyfin already holds another TMDb id for the work, the app asks
first: "Jellyfin holds TMDb … Replace it with …?" — for a series, it says why the link stays in the
app and asks "Link … instead?": Jellyfin keeps its own id then, and the app goes by the link. A work
Jellyfin keeps as two items, two copies of one film say, lists both, and you tick the ones to link. A
show picked for a film — one Planet Earth episode Jellyfin lists as a film — links the work as an
episode of that show, with a season and an episode if you give them; that link is kept in the app,
and nothing is written into Jellyfin for it: Jellyfin would take the show's TMDb number for a film's,
and the item would then claim to be some other film.

**A series' link is kept in the app too**, and nothing is written into Jellyfin for it. Each time
Jellyfin saves a series, it saves every season and episode of it again as well, and rewrites some of
their details, such as their age rating (Jellyfin's parental rating) — so writing a series' TMDb id
there would change far more than its ids. The dialog says so under the series.

After **Save**, each item says where its id is:

- **"Written to Jellyfin"**, then **"In Jellyfin"** once the next library sync reads it there.
- **"Not in Jellyfin yet"** — Jellyfin didn't take it, or the app couldn't tell; the line says why.
  Choose **Retry**.
- **"Jellyfin lost it"** — Jellyfin held it, and a later sync read the item without it. Choose
  **Write again**.
- **"Kept in the app"** — a series' link, or an episode's link to its show.

From then on the work counts as if Jellyfin had the id: its card shows it "Set by" you, with when, and
no longer warns that an id is missing; the Library and Search pages find it by the id; a release
that names the id matches it; the recommendations start from it; and another item of the same
library with the id becomes, after the next sync, another version of the same work. When Jellyfin
holds a different TMDb id than the one linked, the card says "TMDb ids disagree: … in Jellyfin, …
linked", and neither counts until one changes. **Remove link**, after a confirmation, forgets the
link and changes back in Jellyfin the ids the app wrote — only where Jellyfin still holds what the
app wrote. A link kept in the app is simply forgotten.

## The TMDb links page

Choose the **Missing an id** filter on the **Library** page, then **Review works without a TMDb id**.
The line at the top says when the suggestions were last prepared: the app searches TMDb for every
work without an id after each library sync, and **Prepare suggestions** (**Prepare again** once they
have been) searches them all now; it waits while a preparation is under way. The page has four tabs:

- **Review** — one work at a time, with the dialog's suggestions, paste and search. Press **1** to
  **5** to pick a suggestion, **Enter** to save it, **0** when none fits (the work leaves the list),
  **→** to skip it for now, and **/** to jump to the search field. The keys do nothing while you type
  in a field.
- **Confident** — every work whose suggestion is confident, all ticked, and for each library that
  holds files how many of its works have no id and how many of those are confident. **Link N works** links the
  ticked ones at once, up to a page of 100: each is checked again first, and skipped with the
  reason when its suggestion is no longer confident. A film's ids are then written into Jellyfin one
  work at a time — a series' link is kept in the app —, and each row shows how that went.
- **Conflicts** — works where Jellyfin holds another TMDb id than the one linked. Open one to keep
  one of the two.
- **Unattached** — links whose item Jellyfin no longer lists. A link finds its file again when the
  file comes back under a new item; **Remove** the ones you don't need.

## Turning it on

The owner connects the film and TV catalog once:

1. Create a free account at [themoviedb.org](https://www.themoviedb.org/).
2. On themoviedb.org, open **Settings** → **API** and, if you haven't yet, ask for an API key (TMDb
   asks a few questions about your use). Copy the **API Read Access Token** — the long one, not the
   shorter **API Key**.
3. In the app, open your user menu → **Integrations** → **Add integration**. Choose **TMDb** as the
   service, give it a name, enter `https://api.themoviedb.org` as the address and paste the token as
   the **API key**.
4. Choose **Test connection**; it should say "Connected to TMDb.". Then choose **Add integration**.

The lists are computed right away, by themselves, and again after every library sync. The first time
takes a few minutes; open **Recommendations** to see how far it is.

Every user then sees all four lists and the app may run up to 60 searches a day for them. Each user
changes that in their own **Profile**; see [Your Profile settings](#your-profile-settings).

To link works to TMDb, the owner also:

1. Allows the media folders under the user menu → **Media folders**, if not yet, so the app measures
   how long each file is: without that, no suggestion is confident.
2. Chooses **Sync now** on the **Library** page — the suggestions are prepared after every sync — or
   **Prepare suggestions** (**Prepare again**) on the **TMDb links** page.
3. Checks that the Jellyfin integration's API key is an administrator's (a key made in Jellyfin under
   **Dashboard → API Keys** is) and that Jellyfin has an enabled administrator account: the app reads
   and writes an item as that account.

Jellyfin keeps your watch history by an item's ids, and a work with watch history hasn't been linked
in a test yet: link one you have watched first, and check that it still shows as played.

## Changes to the search

**Anime and documentary films are found as films.** Toloka keeps films beside series in its anime and
documentary sections. Until now a release there was taken for a series, so a film such as "Akira
(1988)" was matched only to your series, and the search for a film didn't look there. Now a release
there is a film unless its title says it holds seasons or episodes — "Сезон 1", "S1-5", "E1-31 of
37", "серії 1-4 з 12" — and the search for a film looks there too. "Akira (1988)" is matched to your
Akira, and "Death Note (E1-31 of 37)" stays a series — now read as "Death Note", so it is matched to
your Death Note series by its title.

**Toloka's own title under each release.** Prowlarr shortens Toloka's titles, often to the English
part alone. Each release on the **Search** page, and its **Details**, now shows Toloka's own title
under it, Ukrainian first. Releases found before this update don't have it.

## A wider page

On a big screen, every page is now up to 1280px wide, where it was 1024px. The cards on the
**Recommendations** page are wider too, so they come two a row from a window about 1020px wide
(until now from about 830px), and one a row below that.

## Your files

Recommendations only read your library. Nothing is downloaded, moved, renamed or changed because of
them: a release is sent only when you pick it and send it with the **Download** dialog, and the app
writes nothing the catalog says into your library or into Jellyfin by itself. The posters are kept in
the app's own data folder.

A film's TMDb link is the one thing the app writes into Jellyfin, and only when the owner saves one:
the film's TMDb id, and its IMDb id where Jellyfin has none. No title, image, lock or other detail is
changed and no refresh is started. The cast and crew Jellyfin lists for the film stay as they are:
the app doesn't send them back, as Jellyfin would rebuild them from their names and roles alone. As
it saves the ids, Jellyfin itself drops any other id of the film it doesn't accept the form of. Your
files and folders are never touched. Removing the link changes back the ids the app wrote, where
Jellyfin still holds them. A series' link, and an episode's link to its show, are kept in the app
only.
